          From text to search

          1. Evidence. The text is split into sentences and sections ("Requirements", "Nice to have", "Ihr Profil"…). A skill counts only if a phrase from the curated vocabulary appears word for word. Short acronyms such as SAP or ATS must match in capitals.
          2. Priority. Wording such as "is a plus", "preferred" or "von Vorteil" marks nice-to-haves. Specific tools rank above broad domains. Working conditions, such as shift work, are kept as context rather than keywords.
          3. Vocabulary. Each skill and job family carries the terms people actually write on profiles in English, German (both gendered forms, because LinkedIn has no wildcards) and Polish.
          4. Searches. Synonyms are grouped by category: OR inside a group, AND between groups. Each platform gets its own syntax. X-ray searches are trimmed to Google's word limit, and every search is parsed and checked.
          5. You decide. ESCO concepts and AI suggestions are never added automatically.

          Privacy

          Everything runs in your browser. Job descriptions, ATS files and drafts are not uploaded or stored, and the fonts are self-hosted. The optional AI step sends the role summary only to the endpoint you configure, with your key.

          Limitations

          • The vocabulary is curated by hand (version ), so unknown tools are not guessed. Add them as terms.
          • The talent map is an editorial starting list for Berlin-Brandenburg, not market data.
          • Platforms change their search syntax, so always test a search on the platform itself.
          • The benchmark measures term extraction on authored job descriptions, not search results or hiring outcomes.

          Data sources

          ESCO v1.2.0 skills from the European Commission, used for optional multi-word concept matches. Everything else is ScoutForge's own editorial vocabulary.
