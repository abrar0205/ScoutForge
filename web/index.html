          Pipeline

          1. Evidence. The text is split into sentences and sections ("Requirements", "Nice to have", "Ihr Profil"…). A skill counts only if a phrase from the curated vocabulary appears word-for-word; short acronyms such as SAP or ATS must match in capitals.
          2. Priority. Wording such as "is a plus", "preferred" or "von Vorteil" marks nice-to-haves. Specific tools rank above broad domains; working conditions (shift work, industry) are kept as context, not keywords.
          3. Vocabulary. Each skill and job family has the terms people actually write on profiles in English, German (both gendered forms, because LinkedIn has no wildcards) and Polish.
          4. Strings. Synonyms are grouped by category: OR inside a group, AND between groups. Each platform gets its own syntax; X-ray strings are trimmed to Google's word budget, and every string is parsed and validated.
          5. Human in the loop. Optional ESCO concepts and AI suggestions are never added automatically.

          Privacy

          Everything runs in your browser. Job descriptions, ATS files and drafts are not uploaded or stored. Fonts are self-hosted. The optional AI step sends the role summary only to the endpoint you configure, with your key.

          Limitations

          • The vocabulary is hand-curated (version ); unknown tools are not guessed. Add them as terms.
          • The talent map is an editorial seed list for Berlin-Brandenburg, not market data.
          • Platforms change their search syntax; always test strings on the target platform.
          • The benchmark measures term extraction on authored job descriptions, not search results or hiring outcomes.

          Data sources

          ESCO v1.2.0 skills, European Commission, used for optional multi-word concept matches. Everything else is ScoutForge's own editorial vocabulary.
